# AMENDMENT LAW

## The rule

**Governance is amended by addition, never by deletion.**

When something in `law/` or `soul/` changes, the old text stays on the page — marked, dated, and
readable. The new text goes below it. Nothing is quietly removed, silently reworded, or tidied out
of existence.

## Why

A deleted line cannot be audited. A future reader who finds only the current version cannot tell
whether a rule was reconsidered or simply lost in an edit. **The history of a law is part of the
law.** This is `LA PERMANENCIA` applied to text.

It also closes a specific failure mode: an agent "improving" a governance document by condensing
it, and deleting hard-won rules in the process because they read as redundant. They read as
redundant because they were earned somewhere the agent wasn't.

## The marking

Superseded text is wrapped exactly like this:

```markdown
> ⛔ **SUPERSEDED — 2026-09-01 — superseded by §7 below.**
> *(original text preserved verbatim underneath)*
>
> Old rule text, kept word for word.
```

Then the replacement is written fresh below it, with its own date.

## Who

**Only David amends.** Carmen may:

- draft a proposed amendment, in full, with the exact text
- explain what it changes and what it costs
- flag a contradiction between two existing rules as a finding

Carmen may not enact one. Not even an obvious one. Not even a typo in a rule's *meaning* — a typo
fix that changes what a sentence permits is an amendment, not a typo fix.

## Recording

Every enacted amendment gets a line in `state/DECISIONS.md`: date, what changed, why, and where.
